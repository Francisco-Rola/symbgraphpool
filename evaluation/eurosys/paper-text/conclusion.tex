\section{Conclusion}
We presented \system, a blockchain execution architecture that uses symbolic analysis to predict transaction conflicts before consensus, speculatively executes candidate transactions while ordering is in progress, and directly reuses validated results after the canonical order becomes known. By separating prediction from correctness through concrete access validation and selective replay, \system can benefit from conservative or imperfect predictions without making safety depend on the symbolic model. Across translated Ethereum workloads and native CosmWasm stress tests, the evaluation shows that reusable pre-consensus speculation can remove most execution from the post-order critical path when useful work fits within the ordering window, while exposing clear limits under high contention and insufficient overlap. The broader result is that pre-consensus execution need not be disposable: with validation at the consensus boundary, speculative computation can become committed blockchain work.
